# Back Then privacy policy

**Effective date:** October 18, 2026

This policy covers the Back Then app for Android and iPhone ("the app"). Back Then is
made by an independent developer ("I", "me"). It explains what the app reads, what it
keeps, what leaves your phone, and the choices you have.

## In short

- Back Then has **no account, no server, no ads, no analytics and no tracking**.
- Your photos and everything you do in the app stay **on your phone**.
- I never receive, see, store, sell or share any of your data.
- The app does not use the internet. It reads only the photos already on your phone.

## 1. What the app reads

### Photos on your phone

With your permission, the app reads your photo library (Android: the media store; iPhone:
the Photos library). For each photo it reads:

- the image itself, to show it on screen;
- the date and time it was taken, to find photos from the same day in past years;
- basic details such as its size, to lay it out on screen;
- if you turn on Places (below), the place it was taken, to find its city.

The app does this on your phone only. It does not upload, copy to a server, scan for
faces, or send any photo or detail about a photo anywhere.

You decide how much the app can see. On Android 14 and later and on iPhone you can share
only selected photos. You can change or turn off access at any time in your phone's
settings. Without access the app has nothing to show, but nothing breaks.

### Location, only if you turn on Places

Places is off until you turn it on. When it is on, the app asks for your rough
location (not your exact spot) while the app is open, never in the background. It
matches that spot and the place each photo was taken to a list of cities built into
the app, so it can show photos from past trips to the city you're in. The app keeps
only the city name for each photo. Your location and photo locations never leave your
phone. Turn Places off at any time in the app's Settings, or take back the location
permission in your phone's settings.

### Notifications

If you turn on the daily reminder, the app asks for permission to show notifications.
Your phone schedules the reminder on its own. No message goes through the internet,
and no push service is used.

## 2. What the app keeps on your phone

The app saves a small file in its own private storage on your phone with:

- the photos you marked as favorites (as references to the photos, not copies);
- your albums: their names and which photos are in them;
- dates you chose to hide;
- if Places is on, the city each photo was taken in and the days you took photos
  there;
- your settings: reminder on or off and its time, layout, whether photo access is
  on, and whether Places is on.

When you save an album to your gallery, the app writes normal photo files to your
phone's gallery (Android: Pictures/Back Then; iPhone: a Photos album). Those then
belong to your gallery like any other photo.

**Backups.** Your phone may include the app's saved file in its own backup (Android's
backup to your Google account, or iCloud or computer backup on iPhone), if you have
backups turned on. Those backups are run by Google or Apple under your account and their
terms. I have no access to them.

## 3. Sharing

When you share a photo, a year or an album, the app opens your phone's share screen
and hands the photos to the app you pick, such as Messages or WhatsApp. From then on,
that app's privacy policy applies. Back Then does not send anything by itself and does
not see what happens after you share.

## 4. What I collect

**Nothing.** The app has no developer server, so no data ever reaches me. There are no
analytics, crash reporting, advertising IDs or tracking tools in the app. On Android
the app blocks the advertising ID permission on purpose.

If you contact me (section 10), I see only what you send, and use it only to answer
you.

App stores may give developers general, anonymous reports, such as install counts or
crash summaries, under the store's own terms. Those come from Google Play or the App
Store, not from the app, and cannot identify you to me.

## 5. Data sold or shared

None. I do not sell, rent, trade or share personal data with anyone, because I don't
have any.

## 6. Security

Your data stays in the app's private storage, which other apps can't read, protected by
your phone's own security such as its lock screen and encryption. The app sends nothing
over the internet, so there is nothing to intercept.

## 7. How long data is kept

Everything the app saves stays on your phone until you delete it. Removing a favorite,
album or hidden date deletes it at once.
**Uninstalling the app deletes everything it saved**, except albums you saved to your
gallery and any phone backups you made. The app never changes or deletes your original
photos.

## 8. Children

The app is not aimed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from
anyone, of any age.

## 9. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in the EU and UK, or the CCPA
in California), you may have the right to see, correct, delete or move your personal
data, and to object to how it is used. Because I hold no personal data about you, there
is nothing for me to send or delete. All your data is on your phone, and you control it
there: change it in the app, turn off access in your phone's settings, or uninstall the
app. You are welcome to contact me with any request or question.

## 10. Contact

Questions or requests about privacy: open an issue at
[github.com/njarrar/PicturesFromthisday/issues](https://github.com/njarrar/PicturesFromthisday/issues),
or use the contact email shown on the app's store page.

## 11. Changes to this policy

If the app changes in a way that affects your privacy, I will update this policy at
this same address and change the effective date above. If the app ever starts to send
data off your phone, this policy will say so first, and the app will ask you before it
does.

**Earlier versions.** Versions 1.8 and earlier could also add photos you picked from
Google Photos. Version 1.9 removed that. When you update, the app deletes any copies of
those photos and the saved Google sign-in from your phone. To also remove the app from
your Google account, go to
[myaccount.google.com/permissions](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions).
