# Back Then privacy policy

Last updated: October 16, 2026

Back Then is a photo app for Android and iPhone. It shows the photos you took on
today's date in past years. This policy explains what the app does with your data.

## The short version

Back Then has no account, no server, no ads and no analytics. Your photos and your
settings stay on your phone. The developer never receives any of your data.

## Photos on your phone

The app asks for permission to read your photo library. It reads each photo's date
so it can show photos by day and year, and it shows the photos on screen. It does
not upload, copy or send them anywhere. You can choose to share only some photos
with the app (Android 14 and later, iOS), or turn off access at any time in your
phone's settings.

## Google Photos (optional)

If you choose to add photos from Google Photos, the app signs you in with Google
and opens Google's own photo picker. Only the photos you pick are downloaded, from
Google straight to your phone, and kept there. The sign-in token is sent only to
Google. Turning off Google Photos in the app deletes the downloaded copies. Google's
handling of your data is covered by the
[Google privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

## Favorites, albums, hidden dates and reminders

Hearts, albums, hidden dates and your reminder time are saved on your phone only.
The daily reminder is a notification the phone makes on its own; nothing is sent
over the internet. Albums you save to your gallery become normal photos on your
phone.

## Sharing

When you share a photo or album, your phone's share screen sends it to the app you
pick. Back Then sends nothing on its own.

## Children

The app is not aimed at children and collects no data from anyone.

## Deleting your data

Uninstall the app to delete everything it saved. Your photos themselves are never
changed or deleted by the app.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted at this address with a new
